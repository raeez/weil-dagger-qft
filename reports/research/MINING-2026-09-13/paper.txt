           The Zeta–Weil Form and Nonunital GNS Transfer
                                                   Raeez Lorgat


                                                      Abstract
          The arithmetic terms of the zeta explicit formula define a Hermitian pairing on a
      nonunital convolution algebra. Weil’s theorem identifies its positivity with the Riemann
      hypothesis. A local Gaussian calculation proves a further restriction: the full Weil functional
      admits no positive unitization of finite mass and no realization by a finite-norm vector on an
      invariant ∗-representation domain. The same calculation gives positive Gaussian subspaces
      of every finite dimension at sufficiently small scales. We construct the common graph
      completion of nonunital GNS multipliers and prove its exact domain and transfer properties.
      Closed quadratic forms for individual multipliers need not have invariant domains. Their
      common domain over the whole algebra does. An independent positive arithmetic functional
      remains open, with its possible representation constrained by the Gaussian divergence.

7    Why an indefinite quotient does not give positivity
The Hermitian part of the construction exists without positivity. For a complex ∗-algebra A
and a Hermitian functional W , put

              BW (f, g) = W (g ∗ f ),      RW = {f : BW (f, g) = 0 for every g ∈ A}.

Then RW is a left ideal, since

                     BW (af, g) = W (g ∗ af ) = BW (f, a∗ g) = 0        (f ∈ RW ).

The quotient A/RW consequently has a nondegenerate Hermitian pairing and an algebraic
left action satisfying BW (af, g) = BW (f, a∗ g). This applies unconditionally to (Aζ , Lζ ) by
(1.8). It supplies neither a positive norm nor a class of the missing unit. Under positivity,
Cauchy–Schwarz identifies RW with NW , and Theorem 2.1 applies.
    Let V = C2 , J = diag(1, −1), and

                                             [x, y] = y ∗ Jx.

The vectors e1 + e2 and e1 − e2 are isotropic, while their sum is not. Thus the isotropic cone
{x : [x, x] = 0} is not a linear subspace and cannot be divided out. The radical {x : [x, y] = 0 ∀y}
is zero here. In general, quotienting by the radical produces only a nondegenerate Hermitian
space; it supplies neither a fundamental symmetry nor a complete Hilbert majorant. In this

8     The independent arithmetic question
Proposition 8.1 (Formal factorization). For a linear functional W on a complex ∗-algebra A,
the following are equivalent:
    1. W is positive;

    2. there are a complex ∗-algebra B and maps

                                    ω : B −→ C,         T : A −→ B,

      where ω is positive, T is a ∗-homomorphism, and W = ω ◦ T .
Proof. Dagger transfer proves (2) ⇒ (1). For the converse take B = A, ω = W , and T equal to
the identity.

   For W = Lζ , formal factorization is therefore equivalent to the positivity in (1.10), and
hence to RH. The identity factorization, or a Hilbert space whose spectral data are prescribed
from the zeta zeros, cannot establish that positivity independently.
Open Problem 8.2 (Independent dagger realization of Lζ ). Construct a complex ∗-algebra
B, a positive linear functional ω : B → C, and a ∗-homomorphism T : Aζ → B such that

                                           Lζ = ω ◦ T.                                       (8.1)

Derive the carrier, the map, and the positivity of ω from the polar, archimedean, and prime-
power structures in (1.6), without using RH, the multiset of zeta zeros, or an assertion equivalent
to (1.10). Prove (8.1) with all three local terms and the Fourier and measure conventions above.
Corollary 6.3 excludes a positive functional on a C ∗ -algebra as the target. Any admissible
positive functional ω must fail the finite-mass unitization bound on the image of T . Theorem 3.2
and Proposition 4.2 then specify the common invariant domain supplied by this factorization.
    The resulting representation would still need an additional comparison to a spectral de-
terminant. Equality of functionals does not preserve trace multiplicities. For example, on C
the positive functional W (z) = mz, with integer m ≥ 2, has a one-dimensional GNS space. A
scalar λ consequently has determinant s − λ there. The trace realization z 7→ zIm on Cm has
the same functional and determinant (s − λ)m . Thus a determinant realization must identify
the operator, its domain, and spectral multiplicities separately.
